No-spend January 2027 (UK): a 30-day plan for the long month after Christmas

January is the month most of us feel the Christmas spending. A no-spend month means you buy the essentials you agreed up front and nothing else, for 30 days. In 2027, Saturday 2 January to Sunday 31 January is exactly 30 days. Here are the dates, the rules that make it work, and what to do with the money you keep.

Step 1: write three lists before day 1

  • Essentials (always allowed): rent or mortgage, bills, the food shop, travel to work, medicine, school costs.
  • Allowed, with a limit: things you have decided are worth it, such as one coffee out a week or a child's club. Put a number next to each.
  • Not this month: takeaways, clothes, gadgets, "treat" orders, impulse buys in the supermarket middle aisle.

Writing it down is the whole trick. When the rules are already made, a tempting buy is a quick no rather than a debate.

Step 2: know your January dates

  • Friday 1 January 2027 is New Year's Day, a bank holiday. Day 1 is the next day, Saturday 2 January.
  • 10 of the 30 days are weekend days. Weekends are where no-spend months usually break, so plan them (step 3).
  • The pay gap. If your December pay came early, for example on Friday 18 December, and January pay lands on the last working day, Friday 29 January, that is 42 days between paydays. Paid on Thursday 31 December instead? It's 29 days.
  • Self-employed? The online Self Assessment deadline is 31 January (checklist). Set the tax money aside before the challenge so it isn't counted as "saved".

Rules that make it stick

  • Park the wants. Write anything you want on a list with the date. If you still want it on day 30, you can decide then. Most things drop off.
  • Move the money the same day. Didn't buy the takeaway? Move that £25 to savings tonight, so the saving is real and not just "not spent".
  • Turn off one-click buying and remove saved cards from shopping apps for the month.
  • A slip isn't a fail. Note it and carry on; day 30 is about the total, not a perfect run.
  • Pairs well with Dry January if you're doing that too: fewer nights out means fewer card taps.

Day 30: your review

On Sunday 31 January add up the money-saved column, look at your parked-wants list, and decide what to keep doing in February. Many people keep one rule (say, no takeaways on weeknights) and put the January total towards a savings challenge or a debt.

Frequently asked questions

What is a no-spend January?
A month where you buy only essentials you have agreed in advance, such as rent, bills, food shopping, travel to work and medicine, and skip everything else. Most people write their rules down before day 1 so there is nothing to decide in the moment.
When should I start a no-spend January in 2027?
New Year's Day 2027 is a Friday and a bank holiday, so many people start the next day. Saturday 2 January to Sunday 31 January is exactly 30 days and finishes with the month.
Why does January feel so long?
If your December pay came early, for example on Friday 18 December 2026, and your January pay arrives on the last working day, Friday 29 January 2027, that is 42 days between paydays instead of about a month.
Do bills and food shopping count as spending?
No. Essentials are allowed. A no-spend month cuts the extras, not the things that keep the house running. Decide your essentials list before you start.
What should I do with the money I save?
Move it out of your current account on the day you would have spent it, or at the end of each week. Common choices are paying down the most expensive debt, starting a savings pot, or building a buffer for the next bill.
Is a no-spend challenge a good idea if I am already struggling?
It can help you see where money goes, but it will not fix a shortfall on essentials. If you are behind on bills or debts, talk to a free service such as MoneyHelper, StepChange or Citizens Advice.
