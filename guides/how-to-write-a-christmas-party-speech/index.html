How to write a Christmas party speech

You run the team, so you've been asked to "say a few words" at the Christmas do. The good news: nobody wants a long speech. Three warm, well-chosen minutes is all it takes. Here's a simple structure, lines you can borrow and a complete example.

Keep it short: two to three minutes

That's roughly 300 to 450 words. The room is standing, holding drinks, and thinking about food. Write it, read it out loud with a timer, and cut until it fits.

A simple five-part structure

  1. Get attention and welcome (15 seconds). "Can I have everyone for two minutes? I promise it's only two."
  2. Look back at the year (45 seconds). Two or three things the team did together: a big project, a busy season, a tough patch you got through. Be specific.
  3. Thank people (45 seconds). Thank the whole team, then groups (not a long list of names), plus partners or families if they're there, and whoever organised tonight.
  4. Welcomes and goodbyes (20 seconds, optional). New starters this year, and anyone retiring or leaving soon, if they're happy to be mentioned.
  5. A toast (15 seconds). Raise a glass to the team and to next year, then get out of the way of the buffet.

Opening lines you can use

"Can I borrow everyone for two minutes? I've been told if I go over three, the DJ is allowed to drown me out."

"Before the food arrives and I lose you completely, I just want to say a few words."

"I'll keep this short, partly because you've earned a night off from listening to me, and partly because I can smell the pigs in blankets."

Thank-you lines that include everyone

"To the [team], who kept everything moving through [busy period]: thank you. We couldn't have done it without you."

"Thank you to everyone who stepped in when things got busy, often without being asked. It didn't go unnoticed."

"And a big thank you to [organiser] for organising tonight. If the food's good, thank them. If it isn't, that's my fault."

Safe jokes (and the ones to avoid)

Safe: jokes on yourself ("my one goal for next year is to learn how the printer works"), on the year ("we survived two office moves and a coffee machine that only made soup"), or on the situation (the speech, the weather, the Secret Santa).

Avoid: anything aimed at a colleague, people's looks or private lives, drinking, last year's party, redundancies, pay or bonuses, and anything you'd hesitate to say in front of HR or someone's partner.

A complete example (about 2 minutes 30 seconds)

"Can I have everyone for two minutes? I promise I've timed it.

What a year. In March we moved into this building and spent a fortnight finding out which light switch did what. By the summer we'd taken on [big contract], and in November we had our busiest month ever. Every single one of you played a part in that.

So thank you. To the [team], who answered more calls this autumn than I thought was humanly possible. To the [team], who kept the orders going out on time even when [problem] tried its best to stop us. And to everyone who covered a shift, stayed late or quietly sorted something out before it became a problem: I noticed, and I'm grateful.

A warm welcome to the six people who joined us this year. You picked a busy one, and you've been brilliant. And to [name], who retires in January after [number] years: we'll do this properly next month, but for now, thank you.

Thanks as well to the partners and families who put up with the late finishes, and to [organiser] for organising tonight.

Next year is going to be a big one, but that's a conversation for January. Tonight is about saying thank you. So, if you can find your glass: to the best team I've worked with, and to a happy Christmas. Cheers!"

Tips for the night

  • Speak before you've had more than one drink, and before the music starts.
  • Use a phone note or one small card with bullet points, not a full script.
  • If there's no microphone, stand somewhere everyone can see you and wait for quiet before you start.
  • Check with anyone you plan to mention by name (new starters, leavers) that they're happy to be singled out.
  • Not everyone celebrates Christmas. "Happy holidays" or "enjoy the break" covers everyone.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a Christmas party speech be?
Two to three minutes is ideal, which is about 300 to 450 words. People are standing, holding drinks and waiting for food or music, so shorter always lands better than longer.
What should a boss say at the Christmas party?
Thank everyone for the year, name two or three things the team achieved together, mention anyone joining or leaving if it's appropriate, thank the people who organised the night, and finish with a toast. Keep business news and targets for another day.
Should I thank people by name?
Only if you can name everyone fairly, which is rarely possible in a team of more than about ten. It's safer to thank teams or groups ("the warehouse team who got us through the November rush") and thank individuals privately.
Is it OK to tell jokes in a work Christmas speech?
Yes, if the joke is on you, the year or the situation, never on a colleague. Avoid anything about people's looks, private lives, drinking, or anything that happened at last year's party.
When should I give the speech?
Before the main meal or just after people arrive, while everyone is still listening. Late in the evening the room gets loud and people have had a drink.
