10 texts that make meal planning happen

25 August 2026 · 6 min

Meal planning fails in the group chat, not in the recipe box. The mental load is usually one person holding the week in their head and everyone else arriving home hungry with opinions. You do not need a new cuisine. You need sendable lines that force a decision before 18:00.

These are texts, not a personality transplant. Copy them. Change the names. Send them on Sunday, or on the walk from the station. If nobody replies, the sender still gets to lock two dinners — that is the point of a deadline.

  1. “Three dinners. You pick two. I will shop.” This ends the infinite scroll. You are not asking what they “fancy”. You are offering a closed set. Two chosen, one leftover as the spare. If they do not pick by noon, you pick, and the text said you would.
  2. “What is actually in the freezer. Honest.” Not the fantasy lasagne from 2024. A photograph of the drawer is allowed. The honest freezer is how leftover becomes lunch and how you stop buying mince when mince is already in there.
  3. “Leftovers are Tuesday. Not a surprise.” Name the day. Name the tub. People do not refuse leftovers; they refuse the ambush. Tuesday is a plan. “There’s some bits” is how takeaway wins at 18:12.
  4. “No 6pm debate. Grid is on the fridge.” The grid can be ugly. Four squares, a magnet, a pen. Hungry people do not plan. They order.
  5. “Who cooks Wednesday — named.” Not “we should take turns”. A name. If Wednesday is late sport, the backup is already written: “If you’re late, pasta. That’s the backup.”
  6. “Theme night is a decision, not a Pinterest board.” “Thursday is traybake.” That is a theme. “We should eat more Mediterranean” is a magazine. One theme, repeated, beats seven unique dinners you will not shop for.
  7. “Shop from the list, not the aisle.” Send the list. If they add “bits”, they add a name: which bits. The aisle is designed to sell you a second dinner. The list is designed to buy the first.
  8. “Takeaway is Thursday or it isn’t.” Ambiguous takeaway is a leak in the plan. If Thursday is the treat, the other nights are not a referendum. A named takeaway is admin. A surprise one is the fridge winning.
  9. “Kids eat this, or they eat the backup. Backup is X.” Decide once. The backup should already be in the house: toast, eggs, the plain pasta. A second cooked meal is a second job. You are allowed to be boring.
  10. “If I don’t hear by 12, I’m locking this: Mon leftover, Tue chicken, Wed pasta.” A deadline is kindness. Open questions after noon are how you stand in Tesco composing a novel. Lock it.
