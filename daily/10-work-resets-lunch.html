10 work resets that fit in a lunch break

27 August 2026 · 6 min

You do not need a sabbatical. You need 20 minutes and a done line. A work reset is a fence, not a new personality: close the 17 tabs that are actually one task, write who you are waiting on, name tomorrow’s first job, then eat the sandwich.

These fit in a lunch break. If a reset needs a new app, it is not a reset. It is a project. Do one. Then have lunch, which was the other point.

  1. Close the 17 tabs that are actually one task. You are not researching. You are circling. Pick the tab that is the job. Bookmark the two that are sources. Close the rest. If you cannot name the task in a sentence, that is why there are 17 tabs. Write the sentence first.
  2. Write the waiting-on list. Who has the ball. Since when. The client, the colleague, the supplier, the IT ticket, the “I’ll send it over”. This is the cousin of the home waiting-on list. Work worry is usually unpaid chasing you have not named. Two columns. Chase from here after lunch, not from a browser graveyard.
  3. Calendar: three hard events only. Meetings that would actually ruin the afternoon if missed. The rest is fog. If your week view is a mosaic of “focus time” you never honour, delete the mosaic. Protect one block by putting a real event in it — the call, the visit, the school run — not a wish called “deep work”.
  4. Inbox to named, not to zero. Inbox zero is a religion and a great way to spend lunch filing. Name five: reply, waiting-on, calendar, bin, or “Friday”. Then leave. The unread count is not a moral score. A named five is a reset. A heroic zero is how you skip food.
  5. Desktop dump, ten files, then stop. The screenshot, the PDF, the thing called export(2). Move ten into a folder with a date or a job name. Do not reorganise the whole drive. Ten is a fence. The rest can live until next lunch.
  6. Tomorrow’s first task, one line. Same habit as the laptop shutdown at home. If tomorrow starts with “catch up”, it starts with a mood. If it starts with “send the Jones quote”, it starts with a job. Write it where you will see it: calendar, sticky, the top of the waiting-on list.
  7. Notifications off for the actual break. Twenty minutes. Do not “just check”. The reset fails if lunch is a second desk. If you cannot leave the building, leave the lock screen. A stairwell counts. A walk to the shop counts. The sandwich at the keyboard does not count, even if the sandwich is good.
  8. One done line: what finished this morning. Not a journal. One sentence. “Sent the quote.” “Closed the ticket.” Progress that only exists as a feeling is how the afternoon feels like nothing moved. A done line is also how you stop reopening the same tab.
  9. Meeting notes into one place or they did not happen. The action in the chat, the action in the notebook, the action in your head. Pick a place for this week. Transfer three actions. Stop. A perfect system is how lunch becomes a migration.
  10. Name the first 15 minutes after lunch. Not the whole afternoon. The first sit-down. If you come back to a mystery, the mystery will be email. If you come back to “write the waiting-on chase to X”, you still have a fence. Then the day can be messy. Messy is allowed. Nameless is expensive.
