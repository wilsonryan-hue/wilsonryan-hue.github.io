Quick tips

  • Try everything on the child the evening before, and keep head pieces loose enough to see out of.
  • Use sticky tape, safety pins or ties that an adult can take off easily after the play.
  • Real candles never go in a costume. Use a battery tealight, or draw the flame.
  • Not sure what the school wants? Ask in the class group or at the office before you buy or make anything.

Common questions

What can my child wear for a nativity play?

Most parts can be made from things already in the house. A shepherd needs a dressing gown and a tea towel on the head, an angel a white sheet or top with card wings, and a wise man a dressing gown with a card crown. Pick the part on this page and tick what you have, and it lists what to use and what to make for anything missing.

How do I make a nativity costume for free?

Use what you have: dressing gowns, tea towels, bed sheets, scarves, tights, cardboard from cereal boxes and kitchen foil. Every part on this page has a no-cost fallback, such as a card crown covered in foil or a headband with tinsel for a halo.

Should I check with the school first?

Yes. Some schools provide costumes, some ask for a particular colour or a plain base layer, and some want no head pieces. Read the letter first and use this page to fill the gaps.

When are school nativity plays?

Dates are set by each school, so check your school's letter or newsletter. Christmas Day 2026 is Friday 25 December.

What does a nativity narrator wear?

Usually smart everyday clothes, such as a plain shirt or jumper. This page adds a scroll made from a rolled sheet of paper, which is easy to hold and read from.
